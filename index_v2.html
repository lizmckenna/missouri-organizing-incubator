01 — Why Missouri

A state on
the line.

A decade ago, Missouri catalyzed the largest social movement in American history when the movement for Black Lives was launched in Ferguson.

Today, Missouri sits at a hinge: anchor of right-wing extremism, or vanguard of multiracial democracy in the Midwest. What gets built here in the next two cycles will shape both.

Missourians have forged innovative multiracial alliances across rural, suburban, and urban geographies for generations. The work is to scale that muscle — fast, strategically, and rooted in place.

02 — Theory of the Work

Four frameworks.
One fight.

We don't win by mobilizing harder. We win by building bases of organized people that can contest power across municipal, county, school board, legislative, and statewide arenas. Everything we incubate runs through these four.

01

Stakes × Agency

People who are clear about their interests — and clear that their action will actually change those conditions — show up, stay, and bring others. Stakes without agency is despair. Agency without stakes is hobby.

"The path to operationalizing organized people as the source of power."
02

Leaders Who Organize Leaders

Staff organizers can't substitute for volunteer leaders organizing teams of others. We build for the math: one organizer focused on 15–30 core leaders running 15–30 teams of 500–1,000 people.

"Scaling organized people into a source of power that can actually contest."
03

Bias to Action

People learn to organize by organizing. We push through the uncertainty and discomfort of real public action because that's where leaders are forged, momentum builds, and bystanders become participants.

"Public action is where people actually learn to organize."
04

Stacked Strategy

A school board fight amplifies a state legislative fight. A workplace win builds tension on Congress. We design plans that compound across concentric arenas — building base power in the very communities we need poised to win every other fight.

"The path to impact, over time, at every level of Missouri's political arenas."

The next 3–4 months. Strategic. Nimble. Designed to seize emerging energy and build new bases of power before the moment passes.

04 — The 2026 Fight Back Arc

A year of no.

2026 is an outlier cycle — auditor as the top ticket race, threats to democracy and bodily autonomy advancing through the legislature. The Missouri ecosystem has aligned around a calculated fight back project that stacks.

No

People Not Politicians

A citizen referendum to overturn racist and illegal redistricting maps — should the ongoing lawsuit fall short.

No

Protect Majority Rule

Defending the citizen initiative petition process — the long history of popular democracy in Missouri.

No

Stop the Ban

Securing the right to abortion in Missouri and defending the 2024 ballot initiative victory.

No

Defend the Income Tax

Stopping the legislative referral that would gut Missouri's revenue base — developing alongside the 2026 session.

+

Aligned legislative, local, and workplace fights

Defend public education. Shut down data centers. Advance trans rights. Restore tornado-devastated neighborhoods. Support family farms. Win fair working conditions. They stack.

05 — Where the Work Lives

Across the state.

Missouri is not one state — it's six. Tenant unions in small towns, worker bases in St. Joseph, parents in KC, students in Columbia, faith leaders in the boot heel. The Incubator funds and seeds where the field has gaps.

Anchor Cities

KC + STL — where most institutional capacity sits today and where the next bases of power get built.

Strategic Districts

Senate Districts 8 (KC) and 30 (Springfield) and the nested House districts. Where 2026 turnout decides the trajectory.

Underorganized Geographies

Rural, suburban, and small-town Missouri — the geographies progressives have ceded. We're seeding back in.

Constituency Bases

Parents · workers · faith leaders · students · tenants. New formations for the constituencies the field has missed.
